Polish is not a difficult language so much as a densely inflected one. Seven
cases, a perfective/imperfective split running through every tense, and a
spelling system that is wholly regular once you have spent two evenings on it.
This course takes those three facts as its organising principle rather than as
obstacles to be apologised for.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

The course is written in English for adult and older-teen learners: people
relocating to Poland for work or study, heritage speakers reclaiming a family
language, and teachers who need classroom-ready material they are allowed to
copy, adapt and print. Everything here is licensed **CC BY-SA 4.0**; the site
code is MIT. There is no login, no paywall, and no advertising.

## Where to begin

{{< card-grid >}}
{{< card >}}
**[Get started — sounds and spelling](/get-started/)**
The pre-A1 primer: the alphabet, the diacritics (ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż), the
digraphs (sz cz rz dz dź dż), the nasal vowels, and the penultimate-stress
rule. Two or three sittings, and Polish spelling stops being an obstacle.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[A1 — Beginner](/level-a1/)**
Introductions, the nominative and the first uses of the accusative, present
tense, numbers, family, food and place. The formal *pan/pani* address is
introduced here, not deferred.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[A2 — Elementary](/level-a2/)**
The genitive, dative, instrumental and locative, each introduced by what it
does. The past tense arrives with its aspect pairs; motion verbs begin.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[B1 — Intermediate](/level-b1/)**
The case system consolidated, aspect in extended narration, the conditional,
and the numeral government rules that trip up every learner at this stage.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**[B2 — Upper intermediate](/level-b2/)**
Register, abstract topics, participles, reported speech and argument. Declared
as a partial extension beyond the framework's `core` target — see

Slide decks and worksheets as PDFs, generated from the shared branded LaTeX

## How the course is built

Each unit is a self-contained page following the same five moves — objectives,
input, practise, produce, reflect — with a slide deck, a worksheet and
native-voice audio attached. Each level closes with a model exam of the same
shape as the unit pages, plus a printable PDF. The [appendices](/appendices/)
hold the reference tables you will want open in a second tab: case endings,
aspect pairs, conjugation patterns, numeral agreement.

Grammar is sequenced by function, not by paradigm. A case is introduced when
there is something to *do* with it — the accusative when you order a coffee,
the instrumental when you say what you are, the genitive when you say what
there isn't — and the full table follows only once several of its uses are in
place.
