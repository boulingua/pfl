Grammatical terminology is a second language of its own, and Polish grammar is
usually described in Polish. This glossary gives every term the course uses,
its Polish equivalent, and the sense it carries here. Where a term is contested
or used differently in other textbooks, that is said.

Polish terms are given in the nominative singular. Stress falls on the
penultimate syllable throughout.

## Word classes

| English | Polish | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| noun | *rzeczownik* | A word that declines for case and number and carries an inherent gender. Polish has no articles, so the noun does more work than its English counterpart. |
| adjective | *przymiotnik* | Agrees with its noun in gender, number and case. Normally precedes the noun; a following adjective usually marks a classifying term (*język polski*). |
| verb | *czasownik* | Conjugates for person and number, and carries aspect as an inherent property rather than as a tense. |
| adverb | *przysłówek* | Most are formed from adjectives in *-o* or *-ie*. |
| pronoun | *zaimek* | Personal pronouns are routinely omitted where the verb ending makes the person clear; their presence is usually contrastive. |
| preposition | *przyimek* | Each governs one or more cases. The case is part of the preposition's meaning, not an arbitrary requirement. |
| numeral | *liczebnik* | Declines, and governs the case and sometimes the verb form of the phrase it heads. See numeral agreement. |
| conjunction | *spójnik* | |
| particle | *partykuła* | Including *czy* (question marker), *nie* (negation) and the conditional *by*. |

## Case and declension

| English | Polish | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| case | *przypadek* | One of seven inflectional forms a nominal takes according to its role. The course introduces each case by what it does, not as a paradigm. |
| declension | *deklinacja* | The set of endings a noun, adjective or pronoun takes across cases and numbers. |
| nominative | *mianownik* | The citation form; the subject. Answers *kto? co?* |
| genitive | *dopełniacz* | Absence, quantity, possession, and the object of a negated verb. Answers *kogo? czego?* |
| dative | *celownik* | The recipient or the person affected. Answers *komu? czemu?* |
| accusative | *biernik* | The direct object; goal of motion with certain prepositions. Answers *kogo? co?* |
| instrumental | *narzędnik* | The means, the accompaniment, and the complement of *być*. Answers *kim? czym?* |
| locative | *miejscownik* | Location and topic. Occurs **only** after a preposition, which makes it the most bounded case. Answers *o kim? o czym?* |
| vocative | *wołacz* | Direct address. Alive in formal address (*panie doktorze*) and in names (*Kasiu*), receding elsewhere in speech. |
| stem | *temat* | The part of a word to which endings attach. |
| ending | *końcówka* | The inflectional suffix itself. |

{{< callout type="note" >}}
**Case order.** Polish grammars order the cases
*mianownik – dopełniacz – celownik – biernik – narzędnik – miejscownik –
wołacz*. That order is used in every table in this course, so that a table
copied from a Polish source lines up with one copied from here.
{{< /callout >}}

## Gender, number and animacy

| English | Polish | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| gender | *rodzaj* | Three in the singular: masculine (*męski*), feminine (*żeński*), neuter (*nijaki*). Usually predictable from the nominative singular ending. |
| number | *liczba* | Singular (*liczba pojedyncza*) and plural (*liczba mnoga*). |
| virile / masculine-personal | *rodzaj męskoosobowy* | The plural class for groups that include at least one male human. It has its own endings, its own adjective agreement and its own verb forms in the past tense. |
| non-virile | *rodzaj niemęskoosobowy* | Every other plural. The label is negative but the class is the larger one. |
| animacy | *żywotność* | In the masculine singular, animate nouns take the genitive form as their accusative; inanimate ones keep the nominative form. |

The virile/non-virile split is the single feature of Polish nominal morphology
with no counterpart in English or German, and it is introduced gradually across
A2 and B1 rather than announced as a rule.

## Verbs and aspect

| English | Polish | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| aspect | *aspekt* | Whether the speaker presents an event as a bounded whole or as an ongoing process. A property of the verb itself, not of the tense. |
| imperfective | *niedokonany* | Process, repetition, or an event viewed without regard to its completion. The form used for the present tense. |
| perfective | *dokonany* | A single event viewed as complete. Has no present tense: its present-tense forms carry future meaning. |
| aspect pair | *para aspektowa* | Two verbs of the same lexical meaning differing in aspect (*robić / zrobić*, *pisać / napisać*). Learned as a unit from the moment the past tense appears. |
| conjugation | *koniugacja* | The pattern of personal endings a verb follows in the present tense. |
| tense | *czas* | Present (*teraźniejszy*), past (*przeszły*), future (*przyszły*). |
| mood | *tryb* | Indicative (*oznajmujący*), imperative (*rozkazujący*), conditional (*przypuszczający*). |
| participle | *imiesłów* | Adjectival (*przymiotnikowy*) or adverbial (*przysłówkowy*). Mainly a written-language resource; treated at B2. |
| verbal noun | *rzeczownik odczasownikowy* | The *-nie/-cie* noun formed from a verb (*czytanie*, *picie*). |
| motion verbs | *czasowniki ruchu* | Pairs distinguishing a single directed journey from repeated or undirected motion (*iść / chodzić*, *jechać / jeździć*). Introduced at A2. |

{{< callout type="warning" >}}
**A perfective verb has no present tense.** *Napiszę* looks like a present-tense
form and means *I will write*. This is the most consequential single fact about
Polish verbs and the one most often mis-taught as an exception.
{{< /callout >}}

## Sounds and spelling

| English | Polish | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| palatalisation | *palatalizacja*, *zmiękczenie* | The softening of a consonant, marked either by an acute accent (*ś, ć, ź, ń*) or by a following *i*. It drives most of the stem alternations in the case endings. |
| acute accent | *kreska* | The stroke on *ć ń ó ś ź*. |
| ogonek | *ogonek* | The hook under *ą* and *ę*, marking a nasal vowel. Literally "little tail". |
| overdot | *kropka* | The dot on *ż*. |
| digraph | *dwuznak* | Two letters spelling one sound: *ch cz dz dź dż rz sz*. |
| voicing assimilation | *upodobnienie pod względem dźwięczności* | The way neighbouring consonants agree in voicing, and the devoicing of final consonants. |
| stress | *akcent* | Falls on the penultimate syllable, with a short and listable set of exceptions. |

## Discourse and register

| English | Polish | In this course |
|---|---|---|
| formal address | *forma grzecznościowa*, *pan / pani* | Address using *pan*, *pani* or *państwo* with a third-person verb. Not an optional politeness layer: it determines the verb form. |
| informal address | *forma "ty"* | Second-person singular. Between friends, family, peers, children, and increasingly among younger speakers on first meeting. |
| register | *rejestr* | The variety appropriate to a situation — administrative, journalistic, colloquial. Treated explicitly at B2. |
| mediation | *mediacja* | In the CEFR sense: conveying the content of a text or an exchange to someone who cannot access it directly. |
