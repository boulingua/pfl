{{< kicker >}}PRE-A1 · ORTHOGRAPHY AND PRONUNCIATION PRIMER{{< /kicker >}}
{{< lead >}}
Polish spelling has a reputation it does not deserve. It is not irregular; it
is unfamiliar. The letter-to-sound mapping is close to one-to-one, stress is
predictable, and the whole system fits into three or four sittings. Do this
stage before Unit 1 of A1 and everything afterwards is easier to read, hear and
say.
{{< /lead >}}
{{< /hero >}}

This is an onboarding stage, not a CEFR level. It exists because a learner who
starts A1 without it spends the first month decoding rather than learning, and
because audio without an orthographic anchor is wasted on a language whose
spelling is this reliable.

## What you will be able to do

{{< card-grid >}}
{{< card >}}
**Read any Polish word aloud**
Not necessarily understand it — but pronounce it, from the spelling alone,
with the stress in the right place.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**Hear the palatalised series**
Distinguish *s · sz · ś*, *c · cz · ć*, *z · ż/rz · ź* and *dz · dż · dź* well
enough to write down what you hear.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**Spell your own name aloud**
Give a name, an address or an email over the phone using the Polish letter
names — the first genuinely useful thing you can do in the language.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**Use the first fixed phrases**
Greet, thank, apologise and say you do not understand, and know which of those
change when you address someone as *pan* or *pani*.
{{< /card >}}
{{< /card-grid >}}

## The primer pages

Four short pages, in order. Each carries audio with a full transcript, a
worksheet, and a self-check.

| # | Page | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The alphabet and the diacritics | The 32 letters; ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż; why q, v and x are absent; ó = u and the ż/rz pair; handwriting and typing the diacritics |
| 2 | Digraphs and the palatalised series | ch cz dz dź dż rz sz; the three-way s/sz/ś contrast and its partners; *i* as a softening marker; consonant clusters that look worse than they sound |
| 3 | Nasal vowels, voicing and stress | ą and ę and what actually happens to them before different consonants; word-final devoicing; stress on the penultimate syllable and the short list of exceptions |
| 4 | First phrases | *dzień dobry*, *cześć*, *dziękuję*, *przepraszam*, *nie rozumiem*; *ty* versus *pan/pani* at the very first contact; spelling aloud |

## Curriculum coverage

The primer carries the `preA1` descriptor coverage the CEFR Companion Volume
actually provides — chiefly the linguistic and pragmatic scales
(`preA1.LING.general-linguistic-range`, `preA1.LING.grammatical-accuracy`,
`preA1.PRAG.propositional-precision`, `preA1.PRAG.fluency`) together with the
oral and reading comprehension scales. Where the Companion Volume records no
descriptor at `preA1` — phonological control and orthographic control among
them, which is a genuine irony for a page about spelling — the cell is recorded
as `no-official-descriptor` and no identifier is minted.
