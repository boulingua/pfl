Twelve units for absolute beginners who have worked through the
[sounds and spelling primer](/get-started/). You introduce yourself, ask and
answer simple personal questions, order and buy things, describe where you live
and who your family are — and you meet the case system through what it does,
one function at a time. Goal: the A1 model exam.

## What the level covers

{{< card-grid >}}
{{< card >}}
**Topics**
Greetings and introductions; nationalities and occupations; the family;
the flat and the city; food, drink and ordering; shops and prices; numbers to
twenty; days and times; the weather.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**Grammar**
The nominative as the citation form; the first uses of the accusative;
the present tense of the *-m/-sz*, *-ę/-esz* and *-ę/-isz* conjugations;
gender and the noun-adjective agreement that follows from it; the first,
frame-taught uses of the instrumental after *być*.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**Address**
*Ty* versus *pan* and *pani* with the third person, from the first unit. In
Polish this is not deferred politeness: it decides the verb form in the first
conversation you have.
{{< /card >}}
{{< card >}}
**Aspect**
Introduced, not formalised. Verbs appear in pairs from the point where you can
see the difference doing work, and the terminology arrives with the past tense
at A2.
{{< /card >}}
{{< /card-grid >}}

## The grammar thread

| Strand | At A1 | Continues |
|---|---|---|
| Case | Nominative as the citation form; accusative with a small set of transitive verbs (*mam*, *proszę*, *lubię*, *kupuję*) and with the direct-object slot only | Genitive, dative, instrumental and locative at [A2](/level-a2/) |
| Verb | Present tense across the three main conjugation patterns; *być* and *mieć*; the imperative in fixed polite formulas | Past tense and aspect pairs at [A2](/level-a2/) |
| Gender | The three singular genders from the noun ending; adjective agreement in the nominative | The virile/non-virile plural split at [A2](/level-a2/) |
| Number | Numerals 1–20; *jeden/jedna/jedno* agreeing; prices read aloud | Numeral government (2–4 versus 5 and above) at [B1](/level-b1/) |
| Aspect | Pairs presented in vocabulary lists; no metalanguage | Named and drilled at [A2](/level-a2/), narrative use at [B1](/level-b1/) |

The accusative arrives at A1 because a beginner needs an object before anything
else: you cannot order a coffee, buy a ticket or say what you like without one.
The remaining cases wait until there is a function to hang them on. This is the
function-first sequence the [roadmap](https://github.com/boulingua/pfl/blob/main/ROADMAP.md)
argues for over a paradigm-first one.

## The exam

The level closes with an **A1 model exam** — a first-class page of the same
kind as a unit, with a printable PDF, a stated duration, a point total and a
pass mark. It covers reading, listening, writing and speaking, and it is the
author's own didactic adaptation: it resembles the publicly documented shape of
CEFR-aligned examinations and confers nothing.

{{< callout type="note" >}}
**Status.** The A1 units and the A1 model exam are milestone M2 in the
